Tarang Electronics: your supply chain

Tarang Electronics is a Chennai TV brand that grew from a two-person showroom in T. Nagar into a name on retailers' shelves across South India. It owns no factories: its sets are designed in Chennai and built by four contract manufacturers (ODMs), three in East Asia and one near home. Every Diwali quarter, Tarang's retail partners place their biggest orders of the year, and this quarter 100,000 TVs must reach the Chennai distribution centre (DC).

You have just been made sourcing lead. The CFO wants the best margin on the festive stock. The sustainability committee has begun asking what that stock costs the planet. Your job is to decide who makes how many sets, and whether each batch flies or sails. Everything you need is on this page and the next.

Production costs per model are in the exhibits on the next page. Prices and demand: 43-inch 60,000 units at ₹22,000; 32-inch 40,000 units at ₹12,000.

The profit story

Tarang sells two models: a 43-inch (60,000 units, ₹22,000 each) and a 32-inch (40,000 units, ₹12,000 each). Revenue is fixed at ₹180 crore, so profit moves only with cost.

Profit = Revenue − production cost − freight − cost of capital while goods are in transit

Production costs differ by ODM; the cheapest ODM (A, Shenzhen) is also the largest, but it cannot make everything. Freight is charged per tonne: air is about twenty times dearer than sea. Capital cost is 14% a year on the production cost of goods still on the water or in the air, so slow sea lanes (24–28 days) cost a little more than they look.

Festive launch rule: at least 30% of all units must reach Chennai within 7 days. Only air shipments and the Indian ODM (D, road, 2 days) qualify. So a purely cheapest plan is not allowed.

The carbon story

Every TV carries carbon from two places: the factory that built it, and the journey to Chennai.

Emissions = factory emissions + weight (tonnes) × distance (km) × emission factor

Tarang's freight forwarder reports these emission factors (kg CO₂ per tonne-km): air 1.44, sea 0.007, road 0.0613. Air is about 200 times more carbon-intensive than sea. Factories differ too: ODM B is the cleanest (85 kg per 43-inch), ODM D the dirtiest (150 kg).

The tension: the cheapest factory is not the cleanest, and the fast lanes the launch rule needs are the most carbon-heavy. Round 1 asks only for profit. In Round 2 the board adds an emissions limit.
